# CR-044: Role by feature matrices (ZID-ENG-DEC-007), slice L-7

Status: open (built). The decision record is proposed and awaits founder approval.

## What changes
- **Migration `tenant_0008`** widens the role check on `platform.tenant_role_assignment` to OWNER, ADMIN, APPROVER, OPERATOR, VIEWER, MEMBER. MEMBER stays because a provisioning feed creates it. No new table in 0008; `0009` adds `principal_person_link`.
- **Configuration `config/roles/role-matrix.yaml`** holds the six matrices (68 actions) exactly as the record prints them. It was generated from the record's tables by script, not retyped.
- **Domain `tenant/domain/role_matrix.py`**: `decide()` is the record's decision expression. Every predicate must hold; a failed one is DENY, an unknown or unavailable one is HOLD; approval needs two different natural people; direct role grants refuse self-elevation and any privileged role.
- **Load-time invariants:** only Owner and Approver hold `A`; a Viewer holds only `R` or `T`; an own-account right is held by every role; exactly nine rows are denied to everyone (Zoiko iD verified status, sanctions clearing, other users' secrets, platform emergency access, editing a past approval, reading keys and tokens, cross-tenant service access, ledger history, legal hold). A matrix that breaks one does not load.
- **Evidence:** `tenant.access.decision` audit records (E3): decision id, action, mode, ALLOW or DENY or HOLD, reason codes, policy hash, and the role that allowed it. Ids and codes only. A refusal is committed before the error is raised, so a refused request still leaves its proof.
- **Routes:** `POST /v1/tenant/members` runs the matrix for OPERATOR, VIEWER and APPROVER; `GET /v1/roles/matrix`; `GET /v1/tenant/me/permissions` (a navigation projection, `enforcement: false`). No new event and no new protected action.
- **Web:** the Team page offers Operator and Viewer; the API client types carry the five roles.

## Added in the completion pass
- **Matrix gate.** `tenant/api/gate.py`: `matrix_gate(action, mode)` is a route dependency that decides in its own transaction and commits the evidence before it refuses. It guards the member list, reissue and revoke routes; every invitation (MEMBER, ADMIN, OPERATOR, VIEWER, APPROVER) now runs the matrix too.
- **Role-grant request.** `POST /v1/tenant/role-grants` (ADMIN or APPROVER): Owner or Admin prepare it, the protected two-person approval bound to `GRANT_ROLE:<role>` supplies the second signature, and that approver is checked against matrix A as an approver (Owner or Approver only; an Admin is refused) and as a different natural person. `approved_by` is stored on the role record.
- **Owner policy switch.** `config/roles/role-policy.yaml`: `direct_admin_invitation: ALLOWED | REFUSED`. ALLOWED keeps the Team page behaviour; REFUSED sends every Administrator grant through the request above. Anything but an explicit ALLOWED is a refusal.
- **Person link.** Migration `tenant_0009`, table `platform.principal_person_link` (append-only, RLS). `person_of()` returns the linked person or the account itself; every decision resolves both the actor and the initiator through it, so two accounts of one person are one for separation of duties, and `acting_for` resolves a delegated action to the delegator. Who may create a link remains an owner ruling; the mechanism only keeps the fact.
- **Channels.** The engine takes no HTTP request: a worker calls `access.evaluate` and gets the same answer, a role that ends changes it at once, and a service principal with no role is refused. Webhooks authenticate by signature and workload identity, not by role, so the matrix does not apply to them.

## Second completion pass
- **More routes on the matrix.** The gate now also guards the integration routes (applications, OAuth clients, connectors, credentials, mapping releases, routes), the onboarding plan routes, and the organization-claim applicant routes. Mapping: reads are `e.view-integration-directory-status` or `a.view-organization-dashboard`; writes are `e.prepare-tenant-sso-external-trust-link`, `e.rotate-assigned-nonprivileged-api-credential`, `e.revoke-compromised-tenant-integration`, `e.activate-sensitive-production-integration`, `d.execute-ordinary-approved-operation` and `b.submit-refresh-organization-verification`. The gate is added to the existing checks, never in place of them. A caller who is not an active role holder now gets `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` on these routes (the organization-claim routes used to answer `TENANT_ROLE_REQUIRED`).
- **Pending bootstrap Owner.** `BOOTSTRAP_ACTIONS` (five Owner cells in matrices A and B: view the profile, view verification state, upload evidence, submit or refresh verification, request authority attestation) are open to the OWNER who is still PENDING_VERIFICATION, because company verification is how the organization gate is passed. Nothing else is: an invitation, an approval or a commercial change is refused with `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`.
- **Delegation validity.** `access.delegation_valid` reads the authority grants afresh on every decision: a grant from the person acted for to the actor, ACTIVE, inside its window at this moment. Revoked, suspended, expired and not-yet-valid grants, and grants from anyone else, are not delegations. `acting_for` also resolves the actor to the delegator's person.
- **Person-link writer.** `python -m zid_platform.entrypoints.link_person` (platform operations, dry run by default, `--apply` to write) links accounts to one person with a source and an evidence reference; each attempt writes an E3 `tenant.person.linked` audit record, and a repeat is recorded as a failure to link. No tenant route writes links.
- **Test fixture.** `onboarding_fixture.give_role` gives an administrator the tenant role an accepted invitation would; the mapping test needed it because its second administrator held only authority grants.

## Known limits and open rulings
- **Enforcement is wider but not total.** Privacy, evidence, commercial, communication, search, signals, operator and other routes still use their existing role checks and authority grants; the matrix reaches them as each is mapped (EDR07-09 stays in progress). The onboarding plan approval route (`:approve`) is not gated: the second administrator who approves a plan is an Admin, and matrix A lets only an Owner or Approver approve, so mapping it would change the onboarding flow (owner ruling).
- **Open conflict:** the shipped Team page invites ADMIN directly. The record says an Admin grant is a request that an independent checker approves. ADMIN stays direct until an owner rules; OWNER keeps the lifecycle's own refusal.
- **Approvals** use the existing protected two-person approval, not the A0/A1/A2 engine (L-10); the request is one approval, not a quorum.
- **Links are written only by the platform entrypoint.** Identity proofing, an enterprise directory or a tenant route could write them too; who may, and from what evidence, is for the owners.
- **Delegation** is read from authority grants; the delegation request and approval workflow is L-10.
- **MEMBER** holds no matrix permission and is not one of the five roles; whether to retire it is an owner decision.

## Addendum: the route map (slice L-7, second part)
- **One central check.** `backend/config/roles/route-map.yaml` decides, for every authenticated route of the cell, whether it is gated inline (`matrix_gate`), mapped to a matrix action and mode, or exempt with a kind and a reason (own account, operator plane, protocol, approval engine, billing layer, inline evaluation, no matrix action). The check runs once, in the authentication step (`api_auth.authenticated_caller`, handed `tenant.ports.route_guard()` at start-up because the module cannot import tenant), with the caller's live roles; no router is edited.
- **Complete by test.** `tests/unit/test_route_map.py` lists every route of the cell and fails on one that is none of the three, so a new route cannot appear without a decision. A bad map does not load: an unknown action, a mode no role holds, an exemption without a kind and a reason, a rule listed twice or an empty map.
- **Switch:** `route_matrix_enforcement` in `role-policy.yaml`. REPORT_ONLY (default) records the decision and its evidence (`tenant.access.decision`, object `route:METHOD rule`) and lets the request through; only an explicit ENFORCED refuses (403 `ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`). A failure to decide is a 503 when enforced and a log line when not.
- **Tests:** `test_route_map.py` (22): the matcher, the refusals, the shipped map, the coverage of every authenticated route, and the guard in both modes. EDR07-09 now has a coverage test as well as the gate test.
- **Not changed:** the five roles and the matrices; the existing inline gates; the billing permission layer.
