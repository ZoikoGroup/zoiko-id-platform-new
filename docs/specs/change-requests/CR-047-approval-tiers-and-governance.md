# CR-047: Approval tiers A0/A1/A2, the approval service, reviewers and containment (ZID-ENG-DEC-010, 011, 012), slice L-10

Status: open (built). The three decision records are proposed and await founder approval. Expiry defaults, review SLAs and the reviewer validity period are proposals, not record values.

## What changes
- **Registry `config/approvals/action-tiers.yaml`:** every action classified A0, A1, A2, FORBIDDEN or CONTAINMENT, taken from the records' own lists: nine customer and eight platform A2 actions (Decision 11 section 02), re-enabling after an incident (Decision 11 T11), the four "never, even with signatures" rows (Decision 12), the two immediate defensive actions (Decision 11 sections 03 and 08), and the A1 examples. The file is validated on load: a registry that weakens a critical action, lets a forbidden action run, queues containment behind a vote or lets a customer reviewer vote a platform action does not start. The **policy version** of a case is the registry version plus a hash of the file as loaded.
- **Switches `config/approvals/approval-policy.yaml`:** `governance_enforcement` (SHADOW by default), `default_ttl_seconds` (3 days) and `max_ttl_seconds` (14 days), `assured_review: INACTIVE`, `reviewer_validity_days: 365`.
- **Migration `tenant_0010`:** `platform.approval_case` (the immutable request: action, target, canonical payload, hash, policy version, region, requester person, every editor, beneficiary; a trigger refuses any change to those fields and any move out of a final state or into EXECUTED from anywhere but ready or failed), `approval_case_transition` (append-only log), `approval_vote` (**unique on case and PERSON**, append-only), `approval_execution` (append-only; at most one EXECUTED attempt; unique idempotency key per case), `governance_reviewer`. All five are tenant-isolated and recorded in `table-ownership.yaml`.
- **Independence is about people.** The L-7 person link says which accounts are one human; the requester, every editor, the beneficiary and anyone who already voted are excluded, and a bot, agent, service or AI is never a human approval. A role the requester granted after the request began does not count (no creating an approver and using them at once).
- **States (Decision 10 section 04):** DRAFT, SUBMITTED, PENDING_APPROVAL, PARTIALLY_APPROVED, APPROVED_READY, EXECUTED, FAILED, REJECTED, EXPIRED, INVALIDATED, plus CANCELLED (Decision 12 "cancel option"). The first A2 vote never makes a case ready. A counted approver who loses authority takes the case back down (the quorum is recomputed at every vote and at execution, never remembered).
- **Held, never waived (Decisions 11 and 12):** a case no independent person can decide is reported as `BLOCKED_INSUFFICIENT_APPROVERS` (A2) or `PENDING_INDEPENDENT_REVIEW` (A1). This is computed on every read, so adding a person releases it and nothing is stored that could be edited into an approval.
- **Votes need proof.** A vote calls the L-9 step-up guard for action `execute-sensitive-approval-rejection` (S1, S2 for A2) with `force_enforce`: the step-up shadow period is not allowed for a vote. The proof is bound to the case id, the request hash and the vote, so a proof for another case, person or vote is refused, and an S2 proof is spent. The approver must also present the request hash they reviewed.
- **Execution is separate and revalidates:** expiry, the hash recomputed under the current policy version and region (a change invalidates the case and persists that), the requester's authority, the executor's authority, the quorum, the tenant's trust state, and the executor's own prohibition. A failed run rolls back its savepoint, is recorded as FAILED, and the unchanged request may be run again until it expires. A retry with a used key, or after success, returns the one recorded outcome and runs nothing. A secret an executor produces (an enrollment link) is returned to the first caller only and is never stored or audited.
- **Executors wired:** `owner.add`, `owner.remove` (refused at the door if it would orphan the tenant), `role.grant_privileged` (ADMIN or APPROVER, never OWNER), `recovery.reissue`, `lifecycle.resume`, `reviewer.activate`; containment: `lifecycle.suspend`, `authenticator.revoke`.
- **Reviewers (Decision 12):** an Owner or Administrator proposes an outside reviewer (external professional, co-director, trustee, authorized delegate) with documented authority, an independence declaration and declared conflicts. The proposal opens an A1 case; the appointment becomes active through that A1 approval, or through a platform verification an operator records with `python -m zid_platform.entrypoints.verify_reviewer`. Nobody appoints themselves. A reviewer's declared conflicts bar them from the requests they conflict with. Ending an appointment is immediate.
- **Containment:** `POST /v1/containment-actions` runs a defensive action (suspend access, revoke an authenticator) at once, for a live Owner or Administrator, with an audit record naming actor, action, reason, time and region, a `post_incident_review: REQUIRED` marker and a `governance.containment.executed` event. It can suspend or revoke and can never grant. Restoring is an A2 case.
- **Recovery:** `POST /v1/recovery-cases` opens the A2 case for privileged recovery. It needs a named identity re-proofing reference and a target who holds a privileged role; the person being recovered cannot vote; execution issues a single-use enrollment ticket that expires in one hour. It restores no role and no session.
- **Endpoints:** `GET /v1/approval-policy`, `GET /v1/approval-policy/actions/{id}`, `GET /v1/governance/solo-status`, `POST /v1/approval-cases`, `GET /v1/approval-cases[/{id}]`, `POST /v1/approval-cases/{id}/votes`, `:revise`, `:cancel`, `/execute` (header `Idempotency-Key`), `GET /v1/approval-cases/{id}/evidence`, `POST /v1/recovery-cases`, `POST /v1/containment-actions`, `GET|POST /v1/governance/reviewers`, `POST /v1/governance/reviewers/{id}:revoke`.
- **Earlier two-person path:** owner add, owner remove, privileged role grant and tenant resume keep the authority-module approval while `governance_enforcement` is SHADOW (a warning is logged that an A2 case would be required). With ENFORCE they are refused with `APPROVAL_CASE_REQUIRED`.
- **Events** (declared in `proposed-events.yaml`, domain `governance` added to the catalogue guard): `governance.approval.case_created`, `.vote_recorded`, `.executed`, `.failed`, `.invalidated` (priority lane), `.expired`, `.cancelled`, `governance.containment.executed`. Audit records (E3) also cover denials: `governance.approval.refused`, `.vote_denied`, `.execution_refused`, `governance.containment.refused`. Ids, tier, hash, codes only; no payload.
- **Step-up guard:** `require_step_up` gained `force_enforce` (an approval vote is never shadowed).

## Conflicts between the records, and what was chosen
- **Endpoint names.** Decision 10 says `/approval-requests` with `/decisions`, Decision 11 says `/votes`, Decision 12 says `/approval-cases` with `/approvals`. `/v1/approval-requests` is already the authority module's transaction-bound approval, so the new service uses `/v1/approval-cases` and `/votes`. Engineering default.
- **A2 for a one-person tenant.** Decision 12 says A2 is never waived and offers "independent review or hold"; Decision 13C names a qualified external review route. Chosen: reviewers appointed by the organization (and verified) are the only route; Zoiko iD Assured Review stays off. Held otherwise.
- **Re-enable after an incident.** Decision 11 T11 says A2; the existing `tenant:resume` used the two-person approval. Both exist until the owners enforce.

- **Platform actions (Decision 11, T09):** the eight platform A2 actions are approved in the **platform operations organization**, the operator tenant (`ZID_OPERATOR_TENANT_ID`). Its Owners vote (REVIEWER and customer roles cannot). A tenant that is not that organization is refused (`APPROVAL_PLATFORM_ACTION`). Executing a platform case does not perform the operation, it records an authorization (`platform.authorize`). The operator tool must call `consume_authorization` (action, target and exact payload, current policy version, inside `authorization_valid_seconds`), which uses the authorization up in the same transaction: one approval, one operation, and no approval means the operation does not run, whatever console or script asks. `entrypoints.bootstrap_operator` is the first consumer: in a deployed environment every operator after the first needs an executed `root-super-admin-or-global-break-glass-grant` approval for that account and those roles. Local and test are not gated, and the **first** operator is the bootstrap exception (nobody independent exists yet; it comes from configuration under the deployment's own controls).
- **Machine-checked gates:** an executor may carry `check`, evaluated at request, shown with the case and evaluated again at execution. Wired: an incoming Owner must hold an active passkey (blocking); a role grant lists the permissions it would carry (informing); an agent must still be pending approval (blocking). A gate that stops holding fails the run and changes nothing.
- **More executors:** `member.invite` (A1, MEMBER, OPERATOR or VIEWER only), `lifecycle.agent_activate` (A2, runs the agent registry's own evidence checks and fails if any fails), `trust.adjudication_request` (A2: records and announces a request for Zoiko iD's own adjudication and changes no status, because tenant votes alone can never reverse a platform trust decision), `platform.authorize`.
- **`no_operation`:** four voted actions have no executor and say why, in the registry and on `GET /v1/approval-policy`: weakening authentication, recovery or approval policy (the platform allows session limits to be shortened only and forbids removing mandatory controls, so there is no permitted weakening to apply); credential issuer or authority root change (roots are established by verification and ended by revocation, there is no tenant operation that replaces one); organization-wide export or impersonation (no such capability exists; evidence export is per receipt and no impersonation power exists); delegated authority (the authority module issues grants under its own checks). A registry row that is voted, has no executor and gives no reason does not load.
- **Screens:** the approvals inbox, the case page (review, Approve with its step-up proof, Reject, Withdraw, Revise, Apply) and the governance page (solo status, reviewers) are in the web console (`/admin/approvals`, `/admin/approvals/{id}`, `/admin/approvals/governance`) with a navigation entry for Owners and Administrators.

## Known limits, stated plainly
- **What is not machine-checked.** Most of Decision 11's additional gates are still human review shown to the approver: successor verification beyond the no-orphan rule, privacy and residency clearance, key ceremony evidence, re-proofing evidence (a reference is required, its content is not read). Only the gates listed above run.
- **The platform operations organization needs its own Owners.** The operator tenant's Owners (who they are, how they authenticate) are an owner ruling; until they exist no platform case can reach two independent approvals.
- **Platform operations other than operator registration do not yet call `consume_authorization`**: key replacement, rollback, restore, retention and the others have no tool in this repository to guard. The guard exists and is tested; each tool must call it when it is written. A test pins that the only guarded consumer today is `bootstrap_operator`.
- **Legal and country re-checks at execution** cover the policy version, the region and the tenant's trust state. A legal-form or country re-check has no source. Decision 11 T13 is met for the four forbidden actions (no case can exist) and for `trust.adjudication_request` (it changes no status).
- **Legal form** of a one-member organization is reported unresolved (`legal_form_resolved: false`), never inferred from the member count.
- **No Assured Review**: a switch, off, with no service behind it.
- **Notifications** are the events; no consumer sends mail.
- **Screens are not exercised in a browser here**; their pure logic and the typed client are unit-tested and the backend routes they call are tested end to end.
- **Expiry** is applied the first time anything touches a case (and recorded then); there is no sweeper.
- **Concurrency:** one case is locked while it moves; a vote that arrives after quorum is refused as not open.
