# CR-110: WF03 signal model, one deterministic decision, and the controls around it

Source: Wireframe 03, ePassport NFC, document authenticity and biometric proofing (ZID-DTV-WF-03). Status: engineering for the decision rules and controls built 17 Nov 2026; nothing in this change reads a chip, runs a liveness engine or names an assurance level.

## What was built

- `verification/domain/idv_decision.py`: the signals as separate state sets (optical, NFC access, passive authentication, chip or active authentication, face match, liveness) and `decide(signals, policy)`, which returns APPROVED, REVIEW_REQUIRED, REJECTED, INDETERMINATE or ABANDONED with every reason, the claims it supports and the policy version it was pinned to. Rules: reading a chip is not a verified chip; no trust root gives CERT_MISSING and INDETERMINATE, never a pass and never an accusation; only a broken signature, a manipulated document or an expired document under a REFUSE policy rejects; a web capture never claims the chip; clone resistance is its own claim and unsupported is never a pass; a failed or inconclusive presentation check is never repaired by a good face score; a face outcome never rejects on its own; printed text or a typed transcription alone never approves; printed data differing from the chip, failed MRZ check digits and capture-injection risk block automatic approval; expiry follows the market policy; an alternative route exists only if the signed policy names it, carries no chip-grade claim and needs distinct reviewers; a lost entitlement or unreleased method makes the route INDETERMINATE.
- `verification/domain/idv_controls.py`: biometrics are OFF until a lawful basis, an impact assessment and a notice are recorded, the current notice was shown, consent (where it is the basis) is given and not withdrawn, and a self-hosted engine has an independent presentation-attack evaluation; trust material is accepted only from an allowlisted authority per country and kind; a trust change names the cases it touches; retry ceilings per market stop an attempt and an unset ceiling allows none.

## Wired to a stored case (added the same day)

- **Evidence into signals** (`idv_evidence.py`): the platform's own passive-authentication verdict becomes the WF03 chip signals (no revocation check gives REVOCATION_UNKNOWN, never a pass; a missing root gives CERT_MISSING); the capture envelope is checked against a server-issued one-time nonce bound to the case, freshness, a media digest seen before (a replay), a virtual-camera flag and a device-attestation flag, and what cannot be established is "not established", never clean; the printed-document check results become PASS, FAIL, REVIEW or UNKNOWN (a fatal check that failed is manipulation, other failures and doubt ask for a person); printed holder data is compared with the chip's DG1 after a documented normalisation.
- **Market rules file** (`idv_policy.py`, `config/idv/market-policies.yaml`): read strictly, hashed, and shipped with no market, so a case for an unlisted market is refused and nothing runs on a default. A malformed entry refuses the whole file; an unknown key is an error.
- **Stored case** (migration `verification_0020`: `verify.idv_case`, `verify.idv_case_decision`, `verify.idv_media_hash`, all row-level secured): a case is pinned to the market's rules by digest, its decision is re-derived and stored on every change with an append-only history row, APPROVED, REJECTED and ABANDONED close it (a trigger refuses any change afterwards), and nothing is deleted. Retry ceilings per market are enforced; biometric steps are refused unless the market's basis, impact assessment, notice, consent and (for a self-hosted engine) independent evaluation are recorded, and withdrawing consent switches the person signals back off.
- **API** (`/v1/idv/cases`): open, list, read with history, nonce, nfc-state, chip, capture, optical, consent, consent withdrawal, abandon and a reviewer's signature (`verification.idv.review`, a protected action). No route accepts a result: signals come from the platform's checks. Only the applicant adds evidence; a reviewer is never the applicant and signs once.
- **Webhook** `com.zoiko.id.verification.idv.case_decided.v1`, raised when the decision changes, carrying codes, states and the policy digest, never document data, an image or a score.
- **Screen**: the console page "Identity proofing cases" shows each decision with its reasons in plain words, the six signals, the claims that may be repeated, the policy digest, and the reviewer signature.

## The five open items, as far as they can be built here (added later the same day)

- **Printed-document image checks** (`idv_image.py`, sidecar `POST /inspect`, `image_inspect.py`, `idv_document.py`): the imaging sidecar, which already holds the only imaging library, now measures a photograph (resolution, sharpness, glare, exposure, and a JPEG re-compression screen). The backend decides what the numbers mean. **What exists is quality screening and one heuristic, not authenticity.** No template, hologram, microprint, UV, font or photo-substitution check exists: no result named TEMPLATE, TAMPER or PHOTO_SUBSTITUTION is ever produced, so a market that requires one gets "not run" and an INDETERMINATE decision, never an approval. The thresholds are PROPOSED, were not measured against any document population, and a synthetic test showed that a heavily blurred image still scores above the proposed sharpness floor, so they need tuning on real captures before they mean anything.
- **The platform reads the printed lines itself**: the sidecar's text reader reads the lower part of the page, the platform parses it with its own check digits, and only salted digests of the printed fields are kept (migration `verification_0021`) until the chip is compared, then they are cleared. **The old client-supplied "printed" field is removed**: a caller could have used it to claim a match.
- **The capture envelope no longer trusts the client**: the media digest is computed by the platform from the bytes, and the device report is verified by the platform. The earlier `/capture` route, which accepted a client-stated digest and a client-stated "attested", is replaced by `/document-image`, and the face capture carries its own nonce and envelope.
- **Device attestation** (`device_attestation.py`, `config/idv/device-attestation.yaml`): Apple App Attest is verified as Apple documents it (certificate chain to a root people record, challenge-bound nonce extension, key id, app id, counter, AAGUID). It is tested only against a synthetic chain built in the test; **no real device's report has been verified**. The configuration ships empty, so nothing is attested until people record the app id and Apple's root. **Android Play Integrity is not built**: it needs a Google service account that nobody has configured, and a decoder with no credentials to test against would be an unverified pass path. An Android capture reports "not available".
- **The face engine is called from the case chip route** (`apply_engine`): liveness first, then match only after liveness passes, using the thresholds of the engine the owners approved through the existing engine gate (`self_run.yaml`); it refuses unless the engine is approved for the market, the chip's portrait came from a signed data group, and the market's biometric controls are in force. An engine that cannot decide gives INCONCLUSIVE, never a pass. It is exercised with a fake engine only: **no real face or liveness engine has been evaluated or approved**.
- **Applicant screens**: the web account page "Passport identity check" takes the start, the notice (shown from the market's rules, never typed), consent and withdrawal, the document photograph, and shows where the case stands in plain words. **Reading the chip and the selfie remain phone-app steps** (a browser cannot read a chip, and the face is compared with the chip's own portrait); the existing native shells can call the same API, but no native client for the case routes has been written or run.

## How it is proved

`test_idv_decision` (17), `test_idv_controls` (6), `test_idv_evidence` (9), `test_idv_policy` (3), `test_idv_document` (10: measurements, printed lines, digests, engine scores, the sidecar client with a mock transport), `test_device_attestation` (3: a synthetic chain, every check failing alone) and, on PostgreSQL, `test_idv_case_pg` (5: the document photograph and digests, the approved-engine paths, plus the full approve-and-close path, replay, nonce reuse, missing trust, retry ceilings, consent withdrawal, route rules, reviewer rules, tenant isolation, append-only history). The architecture guards pass with the new modules.

## Not built

- **No document-authenticity engine.** Nothing here can tell a genuine passport from a good forgery by its printing. That needs specimens of each issuer's documents and an evaluation against them.
- **No real device has been attested, and Android is not supported.**
- **No face or liveness engine is approved or evaluated.** The route and its gate exist; the engine, its evaluation, its impact assessment and its licence reading are people's work.
- **No native client for the case routes**, and no NFC read on a real phone through them.
- **The imaging sidecar's new endpoint was run only inside the existing container on generated images**; the image was not rebuilt from its Dockerfile.
- **`cbor2` is imported directly but is not declared in the project's dependency list** (it arrives through the passkey library). It should be declared, and the lock file regenerated, by whoever owns dependencies.
- Active authentication is not accepted on the case chip route; the older self-run route still performs it.
- A reviewer's signature does not change a chip-route decision. Route enablement is not tied to provider entitlements yet. P10 method certification is not built.
- The impact assessment, lawful-basis decisions, notice text, trust-list authorities, decisions D01 to D10, lab evaluation and sign-offs are people's and are not recorded or implied.
