# Signals review guide

For Zoiko reviewers. Open the queue under Signals in the Operator Console.

## What a signal is

A signal is a flag that asks a person to take a look. It comes from a rule, such as many sign-ups from one device in a short time, or from an adverse-media check, which looks for news that may matter. A signal never blocks anything and never approves anything. It only puts a case in front of you.

The queue shows everything still waiting, oldest first. An item marked "Notification failed" means the alert about it did not reach anyone. It is in the queue so it is not missed, so look at those first.

The screen shows hashes, never raw addresses or device identifiers. A hash lets you tell whether two signals share the same source. Hover over it to see the full value.

## Challenge or resolve

Use Challenge when you doubt the signal but cannot close it yet, for example when the rule fired because an office shares one device. Write a statement of 20 to 2,000 characters. The signal stays open, marked as challenged.

Use Resolve when you are ready to rule. Pick one outcome and write a rationale of at least 20 characters.

| Outcome | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Cleared by reviewer | You looked and found nothing that needs action. |
| Risk confirmed | You looked and the risk is real. This records your finding. Any action is taken by people through the normal approvals. |
| Escalate | You cannot decide. Hand it to someone with more authority. |

## The evidence reference

Every challenge and every ruling needs an evidence reference. It is where another person can check what you relied on: a case or ticket number, a link to the records you read, or the name of a document. Write it so a colleague who was not there can find it in a minute. Do not paste personal data into it or into your rationale; point to where it is kept.

## If something goes wrong

If the screen says the signal was already resolved, someone else ruled first. Reload the page to see their ruling. If it says the review was not found, the signal is no longer in the queue.
