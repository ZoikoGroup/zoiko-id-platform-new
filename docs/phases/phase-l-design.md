# Phase L design (taken only from the documents)

> Source: the 22 distinct documents in `zoikoid_2ndwireframes`, issued 28 Oct 2026, all **proposed, awaiting founder approval**. This file records what the documents specify, per document, and nothing else: no choices of ours, no mapping to existing code. Where two documents disagree, section 2 lists the disagreement unresolved. Back to [Phase L](phase-l-second-wireframe-decisions.md).

Document ids used here: **EDR-01 to EDR-12** are the engineering decision records 01 to 12 (ZID-EDR-001, ZID-EDR-002, ZID-ED-03, ZID-ED-04, ZID-EDR-005, then ZID-ENG-DEC-007 to 009 and the records for 06 and 10 to 12), **WF-13A/B/C** the three Question 13 wireframes, **CL-02 to CL-04** the commercial closures, **WF-08 to WF-10** the commercial wireframes and **DOC-11** the ratification instrument.

## 1. Shared vocabulary (stated the same way in many documents)

| Term | Meaning in the documents |
|---|---|
| Identity, Authority, Evidence | Three separate facts. A company record, an ID check, a payment or a login never implies the others |
| Separate assertions | `LegalEntityMatch`, `LegalEntityVerified`, `ApplicantIdentityVerified`, `RepresentativeAuthorized`, `OrganizationApproved`, each with its own source, decision, expiry and revocation (WF-13B) |
| Risk tiers R0 to R3 | Basic, proofed person, verified authority, critical (EDR-06). Operational tiers, not NIST levels |
| Approval classes | A0 routine (no vote), A1 sensitive (requester plus one independent approver), A2 critical (requester plus two) |
| Step-up tiers | S0 normal, S1 sensitive (fresh within 10 minutes), S2 critical (phishing-resistant, within 5 minutes, one use, bound to the exact action) |
| Capability bands | C1 to C5, neutral engineering labels for the five commercial plans (EDR-08) |
| Cells and classes | Existing residency profiles SRP-0 to SRP-3 and data classes DC0 to DC3 are kept as they are (EDR-02) |
| Fail closed | Timeout, outage, missing data or stale proof is never "verified" or "approved" |

## 3. Design per document

### EDR-01 Global launch geography (ZID-EDR-001)
- **Decision:** global from launch; country readiness, not website access, decides what is offered.
- **Waves:** foundation (worldwide discovery only); wave 1 US, UK, IE, DE, FR; wave 2 CA, IN, AU, SG; wave 3 AE, SA, JP, BR, MX; wave 4 ZA, NG, KE and others. No country is live merely because it is listed.
- **Data model:** ISO 3166-1 alpha-2; citizenship, residence, incorporation, operating country, locale and contractual jurisdiction kept distinct; never inferred from IP or phone prefix.
- **Registry:** versioned country × capability × provider × legal status; discovery, registration, individual verification, business verification, credential issuance and enterprise use gated separately; default disabled; effective dates.
- **Provider isolation:** replaceable, country-scoped adapters; unsupported checks never return "verified".
- **UX:** searchable country selector, local document and company-number formats, eligibility notices, contact route when proofing is unavailable.
- **Security:** country permission enforced at every API, job, webhook and admin action; fail closed; audit provider decisions, purpose, consent and policy version.
- **Activation gate:** six recorded approvals before production proofing: legal assessment; supplier coverage; notices, retention, transfers and data location; security and fraud controls; accessible onboarding and support; sign-off by Product, Security/Privacy, Legal/Compliance and Operations.
- **Tests:** unsupported countries show truthful availability; US, UK and EU share one schema; a country can be disabled without code change; gates hold across web, mobile, API, jobs and callbacks; Unicode, local formats, time zones and accessible errors.

### EDR-02 Data residency and sovereign deployment (ZID-EDR-002)
- **Decision:** Regional residency is the production default.
- **Offers:** Shared Global (non-sensitive configuration only); Regional (standard, after readiness gate); Sovereign (contract-gated, not generally available); Dedicated (custom contract, not generally available). "Dedicated" is isolation, "Sovereign" is jurisdictional and operational control; neither replaces residency.
- **Home cells:** US, EEA, UK (wave 1); later Canada, India, Australia, Singapore. A cell that fails readiness holds that customer segment and never silently downgrades the promise.
- **Tenant placement fields (versioned, auditable):** home_cell, recovery_cell, residency_profile, isolation_mode, jurisdiction_policy, key_policy, placement_epoch.
- **Scope of boundaries:** databases, object storage, search, caches, queues, buses, webhooks, backups, logs, tracing, analytics and third-party or AI processors; no hidden global replicas. Only minimal non-sensitive directory data may be global.
- **Failure:** no automatic cross-border failover; wrong-region, unavailable-region, stale-epoch or unapproved-processor requests fail closed or pend; migration is approved, single-writer, audited and epoch-fenced.
- **Suppliers:** must state country coverage, data access, storage, onward transfer and subcontractors before a region is enabled.
- **Legal:** transfer assessment beyond storage location; transfer mechanism where applicable; region-accurate processing addendum, subprocessor list, location schedule, support scope, retention and recovery boundary.
- **Sales:** Regional is included by default for eligible paid production plans; Sovereign and Dedicated are quotation only. Forbidden claims: "global data residency", "data never leaves your country", "100% sovereign", local certifications without evidence.
- **Release gate:** architecture, security, privacy and law, operations, commercial.
- **Tests:** U.S., EEA and UK tenancy, evidence, logs, backups, keys and supplier routes obey the classes; injected wrong-cell tokens, rogue webhooks, stale epochs and disallowed support access are denied and audited; outage and migration drill with no forbidden fallback, dual authorization and single writer.

### EDR-03 Global company verification (ZID-ED-03)
- **Decision:** one paid multi-country supplier (Creditsafe preferred), Companies House and GLEIF as direct sources, D&B as qualified alternative, OpenCorporates optional with redistribution rights.
- **Routing:** GB Companies House then GLEIF, Creditsafe for mismatch or enrichment; US state registry where supported, GLEIF, Creditsafe for multi-state, D&B for complex cases; IE, DE, FR national register or BRIS discovery, GLEIF, Creditsafe where coverage is proven. Wave 2 needs the same sign-off.
- **Sequence:** capture jurisdiction, name, number, entity form and declared relationship and validate the identifier; search the official registry and cross-check LEI; call Creditsafe only when the registry route is absent, conflicts or policy allows; reconcile deterministically (legal id and jurisdiction first, name and address only supporting; ambiguity goes to the applicant or an analyst); create a signed, time-stamped evidence object with source, refresh time, response hash, policy version and reason codes, plus expiry or review trigger.
- **Statuses:** NOT_STARTED, IN_PROGRESS, VERIFIED, NEEDS_REVIEW, REJECTED, UNAVAILABLE, EXPIRED; each tenant-scoped, for one legal entity and purpose. Zoiko issues the outcome; a provider "match" is only input.
- **Fields:** tenant_id, entity_id, jurisdiction, registry_authority, registration_number, legal_name, legal_form, registration_status, registered_address, LEI, D-U-N-S, supplier_entity_id, source_uri, observed_at, policy_version, match_confidence, evidence_hash, expiry_at, decision_status, review_reason.
- **Adapter interface:** searchEntities, getCompany, verifyEntity, refreshEntity; each adapter declares market, entity types and data rights.
- **Controls:** server-side calls; evidence in the tenant's home region, never a global cache; idempotent jobs, bounded retry, circuit breakers, quota monitoring, manual escalation, immutable history, no silent vendor substitution.
- **Procurement gates:** test coverage in all five wave 1 markets; written rights for embedding, caching, retention, resale and processing location; committed pricing; SLAs and exit.
- **Tests:** GB, US, IE, DE, FR fixtures (exact match, close-name duplicate, changed name, dissolved, absent, no LEI, different entity types); no verified status from name similarity, unverified claims, missing data, expired check or timeout; 100 percent of decisions carry source, jurisdiction, time, policy, reason, actor and trace; region isolation under cache, retry, webhook and outage.
- **Out of scope:** director and owner checks and sanctions (EDR-04).

### EDR-04 Directors, beneficial owners and sanctions (ZID-ED-04)
- **Decision:** build representative-authority checks, ownership checks and sanctions screening as separate services for organizations seeking verified status. PEP status or a fuzzy name hit never fails a party by itself.
- **Four separate assertions:** the entity exists; the person is who they claim; the person is authorized for the entity; the relationship is permitted under sanctions and risk policy.
- **Control matrix:** entity (screen and validate status, rescreen on events); applicant or representative (identity and authority before verified authority); directors and controllers (material ones only); beneficial owners (where country, use case or risk requires); PEP and adverse media (risk-based, review on potential match); ongoing monitoring (event-based plus scheduled).
- **Country rules kept separate:** UK PSC (generally over 25 percent or control) and Companies House identity verification; US OFAC 50 percent aggregation; UK sanctions ownership over 50 percent or control, no aggregation across unrelated holders; other countries by versioned country policy pack; no universal 25 percent.
- **Workflow:** resolve entity, jurisdiction, plan, region, risk and policy pack; identify applicant and verify authority independently of the registry; retrieve directors and ownership graph and expose gaps as tasks; screen entity, representative, controllers and in-scope owners; if possible match, ambiguity, missing evidence or elevated risk set REVIEW_REQUIRED and restrict privileges; issue a signed decision with list, rationale, authority evidence, list and policy versions, expiry, decision maker and audit reference.
- **Sources:** reuse EDR-03 routing; a company-data vendor is not assumed to cover sanctions. List families: OFAC SDN and non-SDN, UK Sanctions List (single UK source since 28 Jan 2026), EU consolidated, UN, local lists, each keeping list id, effective date, program scope and provenance.
- **Services:** authority evidence, ownership graph, screening adapter, rules evaluator, case management, evidence ledger; multi-provider, no hardcoded vendor.
- **Core data:** entity_id, tenant_id, person_ref, person_role, authority_basis, mandate_expiry, control_type, ownership_pct, source and version, policy_id, list_id and version, screening_timestamp, match_reason, case_id, decision, reviewer, evidence_hash.
- **States:** NOT_STARTED, GATHERING, SCREENING, then VERIFIED, REVIEW_REQUIRED, REJECTED, UNAVAILABLE; later EXPIRED, SUSPENDED. A provider error never becomes VERIFIED.
- **Access:** only designated compliance reviewers see restricted evidence; customer admins see a safe outcome and next action.
- **Monitoring and refresh:** list deltas at least daily where feeds allow; rescreen on designations, owner or director changes, privilege escalation and high-risk actions; proposed refresh annually, 90 days for elevated risk, plus events.
- **Fairness:** fuzzy matches are alerts; multiple identifiers; explainable reasons; human review; appeal; no AI-only adverse decision.
- **Commercial:** baseline screening cannot be disabled by plan.
- **Tests:** registered company but applicant not director gives no verified-owner right; UK PSC, indirect control and UK versus OFAC outcomes differ correctly; namesakes, transliteration and incomplete records give REVIEW_REQUIRED; list updates, outages, stale data and delisting are traceable and replayable; missing intermediary owner cannot pass enhanced verification; restricted records never reach global analytics, open logs or unsupported regions; legal, privacy and security sign-off of country packs.

### EDR-05 Identity verification suppliers (ZID-EDR-005)
- **Decision:** Veriff primary (P0); Sumsub conditionally approved secondary (P1), not auto-routed until qualified; Entrust/Onfido, Jumio, Persona alternates; Yoti as UK and credentials candidate. A provider-neutral orchestration and evidence system is Zoiko's own. GOV.UK One Login is not integrated at launch; government and wallet credentials are a future separate adapter.
- **UK certified flow:** Veriff's UK-certified service is a distinct product and flow; the normal global flow must not be mapped to a UK-certified outcome; certification of the supplier is not certification of Zoiko.
- **Control plane:** policy resolver (tenant, person, purpose, cell, jurisdiction, risk, assurance, supplier, pricing, prohibited country); provider adapter (server-side session, short-lived SDK token, validated event, reconciliation, versioned country and document capability); decision engine (vendor result, evidence strength, spoof and device signals, human review, policy version, outcome, expiry: vendor "approved" is not Zoiko authorization); evidence vault (reference and signed receipt in the home cell; original ID or biometrics only when lawful, required and permitted); downstream claims only after consent, policy and organization checks.
- **Case states:** CREATED, CONSENTED, SESSION_ISSUED, CAPTURE_STARTED, PENDING_PROVIDER, then APPROVED, DECLINED, MANUAL_REVIEW, RESUBMISSION_REQUIRED, EXPIRED or CANCELED. Pending or error is never approval.
- **API:** `POST /idv/cases` with idempotency_key, subject_id, tenant_id, home_region, country, purpose, policy version, requested assurance; IDVCase fields case_id, provider, provider_session_id, consent_record_id, data_class, policy_snapshot, current_state, timestamps, evidence_ref, retention_expires_at; signed callbacks with exact target, replay protection, ordering and polling; normalized result (document validity, person match, liveness, tampering and synthetic flags, reason codes, reviewer outcome, provenance, assurance mapping); downstream event `IdentityProofingDecisionRecorded` with minimized attributes.
- **Trust and privacy:** supplier proves per-cell storage, access, logs, backups, subcontractors, biometric retention and transfers; minimization, notices, lawful basis, DPIA where required; no training on customer documents; fraud testing (deepfakes, virtual cameras, injection); accessible retries and human alternatives; no silent transfer of documents to another supplier; proofing, authentication, company verification and delegated authority stay separate claims.
- **Tests:** wave 1 live or sandbox tests across documents, scripts, accessibility, languages, low light and poor connection; two conflicting callbacks, expired case, bad signature, duplicates, wrong tenant or region, stale policy all fail closed; global and UK-certified routes not interchangeable; supplier region, contract, SLA, deletion, pricing and no-training terms validated before signing.
- **Not decided here:** which checks each journey needs (EDR-06).

### EDR-06 Required identity checks
- **Decision:** progressive verification by risk; not all four checks at registration. Organizational authority is a separate decision.
- **Checks:** government ID (standard proofed-person route); selfie and liveness (default remote route); residential address (only for statutory, regulated, contract or documented risk need); phone (optional, only where a workflow depends on possession); email ownership (required for email-based accounts, invitations and communications).
- **Tiers:** R0 basic (verified email, passkey encouraged, no ID, selfie, address or phone); R1 proofed person (validated document plus live selfie, or accepted equivalent); R2 verified authority (R1 plus separate company check, mandate evidence and strong authentication); R3 critical (higher assurance, fresh strong sign-in, independent approvals).
- **Journeys:** A basic account (email, confirm, passkey, profile, labelled "Not identity verified"); B verified person (purpose and data notice, method, validation, match, outcome, scoped time-limited attestation, visible states Pending, Needs action, Approved, Under review); C company representative (person proofing, organization checks, authority and screening, owner or delegated review); D critical operations and re-verification (fresh phishing-resistant sign-in, re-proofing only on defined signals, reuse a valid attestation over collecting ID again).
- **Exceptions:** approved equivalents (verifiable credentials, government eID, enterprise attestations after issuer, validity, mapping, revocation, binding and jurisdiction checks); accessibility path for users without smartphone, camera or standard ID; biometric notice, lawful basis and alternative; minors and restricted markets need specific approval; unverified, pending, inconclusive, rejected, unavailable and expired are distinct.
- **No false assurance:** phone OTP, email confirmation, SSO, a selfie alone or domain control is never proof of a verified legal person or representative.
- **Backend:** supplier behind `IdentityVerificationProvider`; purpose-bound short-lived sessions with anti-replay nonce; webhook signature on raw body, idempotency, sequencing and polling; states NOT_STARTED, PENDING, IN_REVIEW or ACTION_REQUIRED, APPROVED or DECLINED, separate UNAVAILABLE, EXPIRED, CANCELLED, REVOKED; evidence ledger fields; claims carry method, policy, evidence version, time, expiry, revocation reference; freshness by risk and jurisdiction.
- **Data governance:** no raw ID scans, selfies or templates stored by default (supplier-hosted protected media, retain signed results); classify under the existing classes; no silent cross-region fallback; encryption, least privilege, redacted logs, retention and deletion; DPIA and biometric lawful-basis analysis; correction, appeal, deletion and alternative-proofing procedures.
- **Commercial rule:** a subscription cannot disable baseline security, jurisdictional checks, fraud controls or mandatory proofing.
- **Tests (P0, ten):** basic registration needs no ID, selfie, address or phone; verified-person claim impossible until policy succeeds; individual proofing alone grants no owner, director or approval right; provider failure or timeout stays pending or unavailable and retries are idempotent; unsigned, replayed, mismatched or duplicate events cannot mint claims; no-camera, no-phone route without downgrade; consent and biometrics meet jurisdiction policy; address never required globally without a reason and customer-entered address not labelled verified; phone not identity proof and SMS cannot authorize a critical change; every claim has time, source, policy, scope, expiry and revocation.

### EDR-07 Role by feature permissions (ZID-ENG-DEC-007)
- **Roles:** Owner, Admin, Approver, Operator, Viewer as explicit non-hierarchical permission sets, further limited by tenant, resource, region, subject, delegation, assurance, lifecycle and policy.
- **Six invariants:** deny by default; no self-approval (alternate accounts, delegation or role combination cannot bypass); verified identity, corporate status, sanctions and trust badges are controlled by Zoiko's verification service, not customer roles; append-only identity ledger; zero standing cross-tenant access (internal access is just-in-time, approved, expiring); user self-service credentials and consent are distinct from tenant governance.
- **Matrix keys:** X execute, S prepare or request, A independent approval, R scoped read, T own account only, dash denied. Six matrices: A organization and membership (profile, members, invitations, role grants, ownership transfer); B verification, trust and credentials (evidence, authority attestation, verified status issuance reserved to Zoiko, sanctions exceptions reserved to compliance, credential issuance, templates); C security and account (posture, SSO and MFA policy, recovery of others, sessions, suspend, own passkeys, no access to others' secrets); D delegation, approvals and governance (delegations, workflow policies, break-glass request but emergency platform access is a separate Zoiko process, no editing of past decisions, role catalogue changes); E integrations, agents and APIs (integration status, SSO links, production integrations, service credentials, privileged service principals, agent grants need a human checker, no secret retrieval, no cross-tenant service access); F audit, privacy, data and commerce (audit read, evidence export request and approval, ledger immutable, residency change request, data subject requests, legal hold reserved to platform, billing, subscription change, account closure).
- **Decision expression:** ALLOW only when authenticated principal, active membership, exact permission, resource scope, policy version, region, trust state, valid delegation, session assurance, entitlement, workflow state and separation of duties all hold; if any is false, unknown, expired or unavailable, DENY or HOLD with a safe reason.
- **Objects:** RoleDefinition, PermissionGrant, AuthorizationDecision, ApprovalRequest, IdentityLedgerEvent with the fields listed in the document.
- **Tests (ten):** viewer API write denied without side effects; admin cannot promote self, approve role grants or take Owner through API or SCIM; approver cannot approve a request from another account or delegation of the same person; operator denied for unassigned cases and other tenants including jobs and exports; customer cannot mark itself verified or clear sanctions; owner cannot bypass verification, holds, residency or immutable evidence; expired delegation ineffective immediately; role or policy change invalidates stale grants; checks work across UI, APIs, webhooks and workers; every sensitive decision has tamper-evident evidence.

### EDR-08 Commercial entitlements (ZID-ENG-DEC-008)
- **Decision:** one server-authoritative, versioned entitlements service. Plans authorize capabilities and quotas only; roles say who may invoke them; trust, privacy, residency, risk, step-up and approval remain independent gates.
- **Bands:** C1 to C5 map to the five existing commercial plans; they are not new plan names or prices; higher bands inherit lower ones unless a documented exception applies.
- **Matrix legend:** M mandatory baseline, I included subject to catalog quota, A optional paid add-on or contracted usage, dash not included.
- **Baseline in every plan (M):** registration and recovery, MFA, passkeys and session protection, the five roles, tenant isolation, audit and evidence integrity, sanctions and trust holds, regional placement, high-risk dual control.
- **Included with quota (I):** hosted authentication and OIDC, onboarding and company verification workflow, ID proofing orchestration, representative verification, basic credential issuance and verification, API access and keys, sandbox, webhooks; from C2 custom domain, service identities, higher throughput.
- **Advanced:** custom roles, delegation templates, policy automation, SSO, custom credential schemas, evidence export, agent identities and mandates, event streams from C3; SCIM, bulk verification, multi-org hierarchy, policy packs, agent governance, risk dashboards, retention configuration from C4; dedicated isolation, sovereign configuration, customer-managed keys, custom SLA as C5 contracted add-ons.
- **Cannot be paywalled:** MFA and passkeys, recovery, role isolation and segregation of duties, required sanctions and identity controls, audit truth, country and residency enforcement, mandatory dual approval.
- **Meters:** auth.mau, idv.check, kyb.check, credential.issue, credential.verify, api.request, agent.principal, data.retention, each with a stated unit and guardrail. No numeric cap, rate or overage is approved; a missing price, limit or definition means safe default limits and a manual commercial decision, never silent billing.
- **Order of checks:** principal and tenant; privacy, residency, sanctions and security policy; role, scope, trust and delegation; session assurance and approval; entitlement and consumption; authorize, deny or hold with reason.
- **Records:** CommercialPlanCatalog, TenantSubscription, TenantEntitlementSnapshot, UsageLedgerEvent, EntitlementDecisionEvidence, EnterpriseOverride. Endpoints: effective entitlements, authorization evaluate, usage reserve, commit and release, usage summary, contract activation. Events: contract and plan changes, snapshot published, entitlement revoked, usage reserved, committed, released, quota threshold, override expired, service suspended; events are not grants.
- **Cache:** signed snapshots cached only within a bounded lifetime; revocation, suspension, compromise, legal holds and expiry invalidate at once.
- **Lifecycle:** evaluation or demo; paid activation after payment or signed contract; atomic upgrade; downgrade disclosed and applied at renewal with evidence kept; quota reached blocks only that operation with limit, reset and upgrade path; delinquency blocks new paid operations but keeps security, recovery, revocation and evidence; cancellation; enterprise override never beats legal, security or residency.
- **UI:** plan page from the catalog; "Included", "Available with upgrade" or "Not available in your region"; usage panel; downgrade impact; checkout distinguishes subscription, provider charges, add-ons, taxes and deployment fees.
- **Tests (twelve):** C1 user enrolls passkey and recovers; C1 cannot reach C4-only actions by any channel; C5 owner cannot override sanctions, region, retention or independent approval; upgrade grants rights only after payment or contract and policy; downgrade deletes no proof, credential or evidence; retries and concurrency do not double bill; quota crossing returns an explicit code; premium plan does not bypass residency; revocation and suspension invalidate caches; viewer cannot run owner operations because of a plan; out-of-coverage provider route gives hold; pinned catalog version, usage event and context are reproducible.

### EDR-09 Step-up authentication (ZID-ENG-DEC-009)
- **Decision:** server-enforced, risk-tiered fresh authentication. Step-up proves current control of an authenticator; it never replaces permission, authority, screening, evidence or approval.
- **Tiers:** S0 valid session; S1 fresh strong authentication within 10 minutes, same tenant and action family, passkey preferred, approved enterprise equivalent allowed; S2 fresh phishing-resistant authentication within 5 minutes, server-bound action digest, single use.
- **Action classification (the backend decides):** S1: change password or primary email, revoke sessions in bulk, routine credential issue and revoke, ordinary redacted export, sensitive approval; S2: enroll, remove or replace passkey, MFA or recovery factor, trusted device or recovery policy change, role promotion to Admin or Approver, ownership transfer, legal-entity changes, SSO trust or SCIM mapping, disabling security rules, privileged recovery, high-trust credentials, high-impact delegated authority, high-privilege agent mandates, production signing keys, critical key rotation, high-risk OAuth scopes, raw ID, biometric, owner-record or bulk exports, home-region change, deleting regulated records, break-glass activation, critical approvals. Invite of Operator or Viewer is S0 unless risk elevates. Risk signals can escalate any action or hold.
- **Methods:** passkey with user verification, verified origin and RP id; S1 fallback to approved enterprise MFA; S2 forbids email-link-only, SMS-only, security questions, API token or device cookie. Federation: OIDC with fresh `max_age`, `auth_time` and assurance; SAML with ForceAuthn and AuthnContext; if the IdP cannot prove freshness, use a Zoiko step-up or deny.
- **Binding:** proof bound to subject, session, organization, device context, policy version, action family and time; S2 hashes a canonical server-side action payload, shows readable details, challenges a fresh passkey and binds the result to the digest; a UI confirmation is not a signature.
- **Sequence:** authenticate; tenant and role; jurisdiction and entitlement; classify; require proof; verify proof and digest; independent approval if required; recheck live grants and payload; execute once; ledger evidence.
- **Objects:** StepUpChallenge, StepUpProof, AuthorizationDecision, EvidenceEvent. Endpoints: create challenge, verify, commit, cancel.
- **UX:** action summary, inline passkey with accessible alternative, timer, cancel, "why required", "Completed" only after commit, preserve non-sensitive draft on expiry, American English, WCAG 2.2 AA.
- **Failure:** deny by default on timeout, stale assertion, missing factor or outage; never downgrade S2 to S1; governed recovery and alternate-device paths; bounded retry and lockout.
- **Session timeouts:** proposed 12 hours overall and 30 minutes idle for privileged workforce, pending security sign-off.
- **Tests T01 to T12:** account security changes fail without fresh proof; no self-elevation or bypass of approver; owner transfer needs S2 and recipient checks; changing any payload field after challenge invalidates proof; same proof used twice or in parallel gives one execution; S1 at 10 and S2 at 5 minutes, server clock; stale OIDC or SAML cannot satisfy S2; outage fails closed with no SMS or email substitute; sensitive exports and region transfers need S2 plus policy; agent or service token cannot satisfy human S2 or second approval; risk escalation forces reauthentication or hold; accessibility, audit and residency.

### EDR-10 Owner and second approver
- **Decision:** yes for critical actions, no for routine. The Owner is accountable but not a bypass.
- **Classes:** A0 one authorized action; A1 requester plus one independent approver (an Owner may be that approver if not the requester); A2 requester plus two independent human approvers (an Owner may vote first but a second is required).
- **Rules:** requester, beneficial actor and material editor cannot vote, whatever their seniority; count unique verified human ids, not sessions, aliases, accounts, roles or devices; approver holds an active tenant-scoped Owner or Approver grant eligible for the action and jurisdiction; no creating an approver and using them at once; step-up per EDR-09 (S1 sensitive, S2 critical); customer approval is separate from Zoiko trust decisions; quorum and separation cannot be reduced by plan.
- **States:** DRAFT, SUBMITTED, PENDING_APPROVAL, PARTIALLY_APPROVED, APPROVED_READY, EXECUTED or FAILED, REJECTED, EXPIRED, INVALIDATED. Execution is separate, idempotent and audited; a first A2 vote never executes.
- **Objects:** ApprovalRequest, ApprovalDecision (unique per request and approver), EligibilitySnapshot (revalidated at decision and execution), ExecutionRecord. Endpoints: create request, record decision, execute, read.
- **Rules:** A2 quorum is two distinct eligible people, neither the requester; no bot, agent, service or AI counts; any material change changes the hash, voids votes and needs a new request; every approver reauthenticates; votes persist only for the unchanged request until expiry.
- **UX:** review dialog (requester, before and after, region, reason, risk, impacted permissions); "Approve" for A1, "Approve, 1 of 2" and "2 of 2" for A2 with "No changes have taken effect"; disabled approve with an explicit reason; governed pending state when no second approver; separate "Approved" and "Applied" times.
- **Tests T01 to T12:** routine owner action alone; owner approves another's A1; owner votes first on A2 leaves target unchanged; second eligible approver gives APPROVED_READY; requester cannot vote; one human with two accounts never counts twice; stale S2 blocks vote; tampered payload or stale policy blocks execution; expiry, rejection, revocation or missing quorum never execute; concurrent votes and retried execution are idempotent; audit shows maker, both checkers, hash, times and outcome; one-person tenant has no implicit bypass.
- **Activation gates:** founder approves the tier framework and the action-to-tier registry is locked under version control; threat model covers collusion, impersonation, role loops, stale votes, cross-region approval and races; operations sets expiry, SLAs and revocation duties.

### EDR-11 Mandatory dual approval for critical actions
- **Decision:** A2 applies to the enumerated critical actions; ordinary activity and emergency containment are not trapped behind queues.
- **Customer A2 actions:** ownership transfer or replacement; removal of the last owner or control chain; privileged owner or admin recovery; privileged role grant or elevation to unrestricted authority; weakening authentication, recovery or approval policy; high-trust credential issuer or authority root change; manual reversal of a high-assurance trust decision (platform adjudication, tenant votes alone cannot issue verified status); organization-wide sensitive export or impersonation power; powerful agent or integration authority.
- **Platform A2 actions:** root, super-admin or global break-glass grant; manual signing key or trust anchor replacement; security-critical or trust-policy rollback; manual restore of privileged identity or evidence records; production data home-region change or restricted transfer; change to immutable evidence retention or audit safeguards; unrestricted bulk impersonation or restricted-data access; disabling or re-enabling platform security gates (disabling mandatory safeguards is prohibited).
- **Not invariably A2:** routine password reset and ordinary recovery; low-risk configuration rollback with no trust, security, entitlement, evidence or residency effect; scheduled key rotation under a previously approved procedure; immediate protective revocation, suspension, kill switch or isolation during an incident.
